How a Small Food Stall Owner Increased Sales by Having Her Own Website

Written by Kocheng Team

Published on September 21, 2025 · 8 min read

For many small business owners, adopting digital tools can appear unnecessary or overly complicated. However, this compelling example demonstrates that even a basic website can substantially improve daily sales and strengthen customer trust.

Real Story: This article describes the common experiences of microbusiness owners as they transition to digital platforms.

Starting from a Traditional Food Stall

Mrs. Sari has operated a small food stall for over ten years. Her customer base consisted primarily of local residents who purchased food directly from the stall. Although sales remained steady, they were limited, resulting in slow business growth.

As with many small business owners, she depended on referrals and did not view an online presence as essential.

The Decision to Build a Website

The key change occurred when her son proposed building a basic website featuring the menu, prices, location, and contact details. Rather than competing with large restaurants, the site was intended to help customers locate the stall more easily.

The website was built for simplicity, mobile compatibility, and straightforward maintenance.

Building Customer Trust

Maintaining a website strengthened the business’s credibility by giving customers access to accurate information, food photos, and operating hours. This level of transparency fostered greater trust, particularly among first-time customers.

Many customers reported locating the stall through an online search before their visit.

Sales Growth and Wider Reach

Over the next several months, the number of daily orders grew. Online inquiries became routine, while event-related bulk orders began to emerge. The website extended the business's reach beyond the surrounding neighborhood.

Accurate location information also enabled delivery drivers to locate the stall more easily.

Lessons for Small Business Owners

This example demonstrates that digital tools are accessible to businesses of any size. Even a basic website can serve as an online storefront that remains available 24 hours a day, shares essential information, and helps attract new customers.

For micro and small businesses, maintaining an online presence has shifted from being optional to being increasingly necessary.

Conclusion

Mrs. Sari’s experience demonstrates that adopting digital solutions does not have to involve complicated technology or substantial costs. A basic website can help small business owners increase visibility, build trust, and improve sales performance.

For local businesses, a basic website can provide a foundation for long-term growth.
